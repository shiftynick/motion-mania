# Lull — quiet by default

A fictional product, made to exercise Motion Mania's narration, captions, and audio checks. Lull is a phone app that holds notifications back, delivers them as one sorted digest when you are ready, and lets only people you choose break through when something cannot wait.

Audience: people who feel their phone runs their day. Benefit to show: interruptions become one calm, sorted moment, without missing the person who needs you.
Deliverables: 28 seconds, 30 fps, 1920×1080 and 1080×1920 MP4s with narration and burned-in captions.

Organizing idea: the lights go down. A loud, overexposed storm of notifications is swallowed by a dark wave; every card folds into one digest, which opens into a sorted list. A glowing ring becomes the boundary that only a chosen person can cross.

Visual direction: bright, cold, crowded storm; then deep indigo, cream paper, lavender light, and gold reserved for the one person who breaks through. Instrument Serif for the calm voice, Inter for interface.
Motion: accelerating arrivals and camera pressure, then slow, eased moves and long holds.

Narration: calm and unhurried, like a thoughtful lecturer explaining a tool to a colleague. Full sentences; no slogans, statistics, or claims about outcomes. The ending is silent so the wordmark can land.
Captions: word-highlighted, from a transcript of the final take, corrected by hand.
Audio: original score. Storm pings timed to each card, a hard stop at the cut, warm pads under the voice, a bell for the breakthrough. Target -14 LUFS, -1 dBTP.

All apps, people, and messages are fictional. The interface is a designed illustration, not a recording.
